# Vehicle paint and reflections

The Java-installed part tree carries `Part.setTexture()` resource identities to each rendered part. The CFG `texture` directive identifies the replaceable paint texture; only the first matching link in that model's material texture table is replaced. Other links, UV coordinates, glass and interior atlases remain independent. Changes update the material map without reconstructing the car geometry.

SCX texture slots retain the base map, secondary authored mix and reflection map. The renderer alpha-blends the secondary map over the base and applies a separate additive legacy environment pass with the authored reflection strength. The MixTASpheremap pass follows `scene_raster3d.cpp` in the reverse; other layered material variants currently use this shared approximation. Reflection maps use their authored UV channel, or view-dependent spherical coordinates for channel `0xfffffffe`; missing generated-map links fall back to the Java-selected global environment. Painted body panels with an unbound reflection slot also use the global environment with generated coordinates, even if the reserved slot declares ordinary UVs. This avoids a zero-reflection hood next to reflective doors on stock Einvagen materials. Bound reflection textures retain their authored UVs. Painted panels blend a subdued environment contribution into the lit paint instead of adding a bright image over dark paint; other materials retain the additive pass. This is not full parity with every original material class.

This preserves the texture-layer structure needed for painted and decorated body panels. It does not implement the remaining native decal-painting/compositing operations.

The **Real reflections** checkbox enables an experimental local cubemap in both garage and driving graphics controls. It captures six 128-pixel faces around the active focus at most four times per second, hides the vehicles during capture to avoid feedback, and supplies the result to reflective vehicle materials. It replaces the legacy reflection pass while enabled, with environment intensity 0.5. One probe is shared by the vehicles; other cars and self-reflections are not captured. It costs additional GPU work and is disabled by default. Disabling it restores the previous environment and releases the cubemap.


Stock Einvagen door/chassis material type `0x15` is `CMaterial_MixTASpecularSpheremapMasked`. The installed build's constructor at `0042a940` sets type `0x15` and vtable `0059446c`; its draw routine at `00432040` binds the secondary atlas again and uses complemented replicated alpha (`COLORARG1 = 0x32`) in the final environment combination. The renderer retains this mask for both legacy and local environment contributions. The atlas RGB/paint blend runs in encoded sRGB before returning to linear lighting, avoiding bright low-alpha fringes from a modern linear crossfade. This is an adapter for the original material, not a replacement atlas.

Additional CFG renders retain their `flags` activation mask. Lamp lens meshes remain visible with all lamps off; city/brake/reverse/indicator overlays and glows stay hidden until their channel is active. Brake and reverse channels follow the active driving controls and transmission. Running-light/indicator commands are not wired yet and remain off by default.
# Vehicle lamp halos

Conditional CFG renders can reference RPK type 13 spot/fakespot lights rather than SCX geometry. The part factory preserves their `lf_texture`, `lf_glow_color` (A,R,G,B), direction, cone, size and offset instead of discarding them as meshless resources. The world renderer draws an additive, camera-facing halo using the first glow tile of the original PTX color/alpha atlas. Its containing group follows the same CFG activation flags as the lamp overlay and the same part transform when detached. Preview inventories do not create these effects.

PNG and other browser-decodable replacements retain their image alpha. For `lf_texturemode 2`, the main glow is selected from the upper-left quadrant of these atlases; standalone images use the full texture. The halo fades outside the source viewing cone, uses scene depth and releases its GPU texture/material with the part. A small geometry-based lens clearance prevents the lens itself hiding its own halo. Glow size currently uses a bounded perspective approximation of the native screen-size setting. This does not yet reproduce other texture-mode layouts, flare rays/ghosts, all color-type modes, or spot-light illumination of the road.

## Normals on deformable parts

Vehicle panel, saved-damage, hood-lab and traffic deformation preserve authored SCX normal continuity. A per-geometry cache groups duplicated vertices only when their original position and normal agree, keeping hard edges and opposing shell layers separate. Each face transports its authored normals through its local deformation basis, then accumulates them within those groups. The unchanged shape retains authored shading; rigid motion and centimetre-to-metre conversion rotate normals consistently. Repair restores the exact reference normals. Topology and UVs are unchanged. Connections are cached before the first deformation; no vertex welding or grouping runs per frame.
