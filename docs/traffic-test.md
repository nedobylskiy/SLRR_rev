# Physical traffic bot experiment

On the `traffic` branch, **Traffic cars** in **Graphics / map debug** selects an integer population from 0 to 50 (default 2). **Traffic physics radius** ranges from 5 to 200 m (default 30 m). **Traffic spawn radius** ranges from 25 to 500 m (default 100 m). In Java scenes, model identities and relative density weights come from that ground’s actual `addTrafficN` calls. Direct map preview without Java declarations retains `cars/traffic/Pickup.rpk:0x0006` as the experiment fallback. Each model is cached once per asset set/RPK resource; lamp images and the engine sample are shared, with a warm reusable pool up to the highest requested count. Lowering the count deactivates, hides and mutes excess slots. Maps without declared TRC road data receive no guessed graph.

Cars spawn on the nearest free cached samples of connected graph lanes within the smaller of **Traffic spawn radius** and the map **Streaming radius**, around the player car (or primary streaming/camera focus in preview). New spawns normally stay at least 15 metres from the focus, reduced for very small stream radii, and keep at least a 14-metre gap from occupied positions, increased for long buses/trucks using their actual hull extents. The spawn limit does not teleport existing cars: they may drive farther and remain until leaving the larger map streaming radius. Cars leaving the stream radius are hidden and their slots recycled near the current focus; one refill every 200 ms spreads work over frames. Terminal routes recycle when safely outside the near physics zone. If the graph offers too few free spawn positions, the live counter shows the lower available population and retries.

Distant **puppets** use graph navigation, signals, following, wheel/flare animation and quiet engine sound, with no suspension, rigid-body integration or map/contact solving. Terrain height is sampled once per movement tick. At the configured near radius, the same persistent body switches to one rigid body/four-spring traffic physics. Cruising cars return to puppets beyond radius + 10 m to avoid threshold chatter; active collision/recovery remains physical until normal cruise resumes. The transition itself preserves pose, speed and dents. Recycling an out-of-range slot is a new traffic instance: private damaged skins are released and the shared undamaged template restored.

## Navigation and behavior

- Roads run **red → green**, opposite stored segment order. At an approaching crossing, choose a random outgoing branch once, then a lane within that branch.
- Connections require explicit road links in the same crossing record and matching grey-lane endpoints within 3 cm. Grey Hermite lanes are also followed in reverse. An unsupported exit stops the bot.
- For near physical cars, the spline supplies steering targets and never assigns their body position during driving or recovery. Distant puppets instead follow the graph kinematically. Progress uses planar projection because navigation Y is not body or road-surface Y.
- `Bot` controls throttle, brake and steering at 60 Hz. Initial target speed is sampled from 15–35 km/h for each streamed car. After leaving each crossing, a fresh target is sampled uniformly: 60–100 km/h for gold/pool A, 15–35 km/h for pink/pool B, reduced in turns or behind another car. These are targets, not hard velocity clamps: slopes and impacts can exceed them.
- An impact enters physical response mode. After remaining nearly stationary for one second, the bot accelerates toward the closest connected road at a target up to 30 km/h. Once close and aligned, it resumes cruise.
- Attempting to drive without advancing for two seconds triggers **3.5 seconds of reverse with full left or right steering**, then another recovery attempt. An ordinary road queue does not count as being stuck. Inside a crossing (including the first body-length of its exit), three stationary seconds can also trigger recovery when following has reduced the target to zero. Reverse requires ten metres of rear bumper clearance in the oriented vehicle corridor, including the player; a newly blocked rear corridor holds the brake and pauses the reverse timer.
- Physical steering samples across the incoming road, selected crossing and outgoing lane, smoothing the steering guide without changing the source graph. A rear-axle pure-pursuit controller uses each vehicle's wheelbase and maximum steering angle, with steering changes capped at 2.5 normalized units per second. Curvature preview reduces speed before tight bends. Long vehicles have up to four metres of endpoint handoff tolerance; signal permission still gates entry. Physical driving never assigns a new body pose.
- After a stopped queue or intersection wait clears, each bot samples a fresh **500–1500 ms reaction delay** once. It holds the brake and brake lamps throughout, cancels the countdown if blocked again, and does not delay a car already moving. The delay uses simulation ticks so hidden-tab pauses do not consume it. At STOP/YIELD this is additional to the five-second stop.
- A car stationary for more than 60 simulated seconds enters **ram** mode: it drives forward toward the route at a target of 30 km/h, overriding queue and signal waits, including a blocked reverse/recovery attempt. Moving more than one metre from the stationary anchor or reaching 0.5 m/s resets the wait. Contact enters the normal impact/settle/recovery sequence and resets the timer; the same body and damage are retained. Paused tabs do not accumulate this timer.
- All traffic cars see one another and the active player's full vehicle. Following uses a 3-metre standstill gap plus 1.2 seconds of current speed, the leader's forward speed and a braking-distance constraint. Oriented hull extents define the lane corridor. The faster car slows behind the first and accelerates again when its path clears.
- Each car owns its route cursor and independently chooses crossing exits. Stream spawns use a specific connected road ID, avoiding a full-map nearest-road scan when rebinding a recycled slot.
- Cars advance on one shared 60 Hz schedule. Pair contact solving runs only when at least one car has near physics enabled. Puppet-only pairs use following/braking without collision solving.

## Vehicle, damage and sound

Traffic model groups (including lamp sprites) are culled against the primary camera frustum. Active cars also supply one conservative world-space bounding box each to the existing asynchronous map Hi-Z pipeline; cars do not become depth occluders. Physics, routing and audio continue while color rendering is culled, and shadow passes retain their casters. Completed hidden verdicts persist between passes while the car remains within its tested, motion-padded box; moving outside that box invalidates the verdict and rejects late readbacks. The inspection camera displays primary-camera culling; **Inspect all resident instances** bypasses it. Hi-Z boxes and traffic visibility counters include these cars.

`TrafficVehicle` uses one `RigidBody3D` and four terrain ray springs from the original inline CFG wheels. Tyre forces, wheel steering/spin and suspension movement drive the model. Cruise performs reduced map contact checks; impact/recovery/reverse use all hull corners. The same body, velocity and damage survive mode changes.

Damage uses one coarse 3×3×5 displacement field across the body, updated on impacts. It does not simulate individual panels, mounts or detachment. The collision hull remains conservative and undeformed. This shares the normal rigid-body/contact core, but is not the full assembled-car soft-body solver. `FullVehicleBotAdapter` lets the controller drive an already assembled `Vehicle3D`; regular Java `addTrafficN` declarations are connected to the stream. Individual Java Bot `addTrafficCar` / notification / racer spawning remains separate and unimplemented.

A quiet positional engine loop uses simplified RPM and a low-pass filter. There are no tyre tracks, smoke or skid sounds. Playback requires a browser audio gesture.

Original CFG lamp resources produce depth-tested flare sprites, including front/roof lamps and rear running/brake/reverse lamps. Running lamps follow night time, brake/reverse follow bot inputs. They create no spotlights, projected beams, gobo, or shadow maps. See [lamp and signal research](traffic-signals-research.md).

Immutable model geometry/materials, decoded engine audio and road curves are cached. New instances clone the scene hierarchy; their body geometry is copied only on first damage. Each instance owns its physics and crush field. Scene changes release instances; Viewer disposal releases templates and audio. Hidden tabs pause advancement without later fast-forward; one update advances at most 0.25 seconds.

## Controls and validation

**Respawn traffic around player** replaces the active population around the current streaming focus using cached assets. **Inspect traffic** focuses the independent camera once between the first two active cars without changing the streaming camera. **Test traffic impact** applies a test impulse to A. Each car's status includes cruise target, mode, actual speed, RPM, road, selected exit, crossings, damage version and position.

Traffic signals use a shared 10-second green / 3-second yellow cycle, with all other approaches red. Each incoming branch governs all its lanes, and a car already past the entry line continues. A moving driver approaching within 15 metres (or three seconds at current speed) chooses once per yellow phase: 50% brake, 50% a 20% target-speed boost (capped at cruise + 10 km/h). Stopped cars remain stopped on yellow. Following another car still overrides acceleration; red blocks cars that have not entered. Missing-lamp approaches at a regulated crossing use a five-second STOP fallback. Explicit STOP/YIELD fixtures also wait five stopped seconds, without crossing-traffic checks. Stock road-marking identification remains pending; see [signal research and policy](traffic-signals-research.md).

Regular original Java population registration/clearing and host streaming are implemented. The slider controls the global active cap independently of the Java density weights. Original traffic persistence and individual Java Bot registration/notifications remain unimplemented. See [Java traffic models](java-traffic-models.md).

- Tests cover reverse road/crossing traversal, explicit topology, stable random branch selection, unsupported exits and bounded long frames.
- Bot tests cover stopped-time recovery without teleportation, the 3.5-second reverse maneuver, queuing, physical spring support/driving, impact deformation and independent damage with shared graph data.
- A physical two-car test checks that the 30 km/h car catches the 15 km/h car without contact, maintains more than 2 m bumper clearance and accelerates above 25 km/h after the lane clears. Navigation tests cover upstream placement across a crossing and independent branch selection.
- A physical City-graph run on flat test terrain traversed six crossings in 300 simulated seconds, with maximum planar route deviation about 1.5 m and straight-road speed near 15 km/h. This validates navigation/steering, not every real City obstacle.

Traffic wheel yaw beyond ±5° activates that side’s original CFG indicator channel (16 left / 32 right), blinking at 1 Hz with 50% duty while physically moving forward. Indicators also work in daylight and do not activate when stopped or reversing. A new indication starts lit, with 0.8 seconds of retention after the wheel angle drops below the threshold. Stock Pickup has only front indicators in its CFG; missing rear indicators reuse rear reverse/running-lamp anchors as amber sprites without changing those channels.

Every 20 simulated seconds, a cruising, forward-moving car rolls a 10% lane-change chance. It selects only the immediately adjacent parallel lane in the same road branch and direction, with sufficient room before the crossing. Nearby cars in the destination corridor prevent the maneuver. The steering target blends smoothly over at least 25 metres (or 3.5 seconds at current speed); no physical body teleport occurs. Turns and lane changes retain normal obstacle following and signal braking.

## Player traffic control

The **Traffic controls my car** checkbox is off by default. Enable it while driving on a map with a road graph. It binds the current full Vehicle3D to the nearest lane without resetting damage, pose or velocity. Bot supplies controls once per existing physics tick; the vehicle’s normal solver, suspension, drivetrain, audio and damage continue. The driver operates gears/clutch/start as needed, obeys the shared signal controller and follows nearby test traffic. Initial target is 25 km/h, then crossing speed regimes apply. Disable the checkbox to return immediately to player input; leaving driving also removes the driver. Original gearbox automatic/manual mode is restored on release.

Crossing speed changes are ramped from the actual forward speed at the exit: the cruise setpoint increases at up to 0.8 m/s² or decreases at up to 1.2 m/s², without overshooting the selected target. Signal/queue/obstacle braking is applied after this cruise ramp and can immediately reduce the desired speed. This policy is shared by traffic vehicles and player-car traffic control.

Spawning first prefers free lane samples fully outside the primary/player camera frustum, using a conservative sphere covering the whole traffic model plus a 0.5 m margin. Within that preference, the closest free point wins. If no offscreen free point exists inside the configured spawn radius, spawning falls back to the closest visible free point. The independent inspection camera does not alter this choice; no extra depth/occlusion pass is performed.
