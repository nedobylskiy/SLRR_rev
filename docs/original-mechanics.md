# Standard Baiern units: assembly and effects of damage

The parameters of this stock car measured in the original game, the engine graph and the Test Track results are saved in [Baiern CoupeSport 2.5 standard](../benchmarks/baiern-coupesport-2.5/README.md). `npm run report:stock-benchmark` shows side by side game indicators, parameters of assembled parts and the current calculation curve; `npm run measure:stock-drive` reproduces linear acceleration. Conflicting interface indicators are noted in the standard.

Tested on a local modified Steam installation of SLRR. The **Car and Obstacle** mode assembles the Baiern CoupeSport (`cars/racers/Baiern.rpk`, resource `0x06`) from **72 objects** without compatibility errors: the previous 33 body, wheel and interior objects plus 39 objects selected through `addStockParts` (including 20 engine components). This set has not yet been tested for other machines. Game assets are read from the local installation disk and are not added to Git.

## Where does the composition come from?

`cars/racers/Baiern_data/scripts/src/Baiern_CoupeSport.java` inherits from `Baiern_models.java`. Its `addStockParts` specifies a standard engine and four struts, four springs, four shock absorbers, four brakes and two stabilizers. The initial choice was `desc.power = 1` (standard configuration branch) and `MODEL_COUPESPORT`. Java source branches are selected by a limited expression reader; `RID` is resolved through RPK dependencies, fastenings are resolved through `slot`, `attach`, `compatible`, then `addStockParts` engine parts are read recursively. It does not implement Java constructors, native functions, random branches, or the general game lifecycle. Unknown conditionals are not executed; Uninstalled parts are displayed as a warning.

RPK's selected front shock resource (`parts/running_gear` `0xFD`) points to `shock_absorber_Ishima_WRY_front`, despite Baiern's comment in the script. The actual resource and its `damping = 3300` are taken, not the name from the comment. The rear ones give `3000`. Stock CoupeSport springs set `kgfPmm2NPm(7.3)` = **71,613 N/m** front and **73,575 N/m** rear; `restlength = Inch2Meter(8.5)` = **0.2159 m**. For these simple expressions, an arithmetic parser is used with a whitelist of unit conversions; the original Java is not executed.

The numbering of the slots of the original CFG connects the nodes to the corners: `121–124` struts, `301–304` shock absorbers, `311–314` springs, `111–114` brakes. For visible mounts `slottype 201–204` and `301–304`, the corresponding `wheel` frame and local CFG slot offset are used. The strut script additionally calls `the_car.setSlotPos(101+wheelID, …)` for the wheel and `setSlotPos(111+wheelID, …)` for the brake; these provisions now apply to the same slots and to the physical point of the wheel. This rule reads the raw Java numeric arguments and does not contain Baiern coordinates. For now, it is the static form of the call with `new Vector3` and `wheelID` that is supported; expressions of other mods calculated during the game will require script execution.

For the spring and shock absorber, the parser stores `bonepos` chassis slots and a pair of `linked` from CFG parts. For example, `linked 301 501` at the front spring points to the lower **shock absorber** slot `301`, although the spring itself is installed in the `311` slot. The visual axes of both SCXs go from the bottom point indicated by `linked` to the top `bonepos`; The length of the meshes is adjusted to their own Y range. This way the coils wrap around the shock absorber and follow its slope. This is a general rule based on CFG and SCX data, with no car-specific constants. Previously, we additionally added `setArm` to the spring installation point and got springs inside the machine; this is the internal physical point of the lever, not the coordinate of the visible attachment. `setHub/setArm` are still read from the Java profile, but their exact native semantics and hinge connections are not restored. The movement of the wheels is currently being calculated by four beam supports.

## State and movement

Each installed part has its own state `condition` from 100% to 0%. The load upon impact is received by the nodes close to the contact; the engine is partially covered by the body in the shock transfer model. The zero state of the strut leaves it on the chassis, but frees the wheel. The zero state of the block frees the engine **entirely**: all nested nodes remain in its attachment tree. The zero state of the internal node stops its function without breaking the engine into separate physical bodies.

| Component | Behavior in the current model |
|---|---|
|Strut and spring|The rigidity of the support is scaled by their condition; at 0% spring the support stops holding the wheel. At 0% strut, the wheel separates and falls, the strut remains on the car. Wheel misalignment appears when the condition is **below 10%** of the strut/support.|
|Shock absorber|The damping from the selected Java class is multiplied by the state.|
|Stabilizer|The original `force/damping` resists the different travel of the left and right axle wheels; damage reduces this moment.|
|Brake|The braking force of a given wheel is multiplied by the condition of the brake and strut.|
|Engine block, crankshaft, cylinder head, transmission, clutch, fuel rail|Available drive torque is limited by the weakest required component. At 0% condition, any of these disables propulsion; at 0% engine-block condition, the entire powerplant detaches as one body.|

6 cylinders, diameter 80 mm and stroke 90 mm (**2.714 l**); out of the box - five gears, first `3.58`, main gear `3.03`, rear wheel drive `DT_RWD`. Now the power plant has its own speed, inertia, clutch load and engine braking. The wheels have independent angular speeds; the difference between the peripheral speed of the wheel and the speed of the contact patch creates longitudinal force and slip. Longitudinal and lateral forces consume the total grip reserve; lateral reaction takes priority when skidding under gas. The straight-line thrust limit remains the same. The car appears in neutral: `E` turns on the first, `Q` turns on the rear, `N` turns on neutral again. After selecting the first one, the standard automatic transmission switches automatically; Repeated `Q/E` allows you to manually select a gear, `Shift` opens the clutch. `W/S` in automatic mode change direction after deceleration if the gear is already engaged. The HUD shows gear, rpm, boost and slip.

The gearbox type (`TT_AUTO`, `TT_MANUAL`, `TT_SEMIAUTO`), gears `ratio[1..7]`, main pair `end_ratio` and drive are read from the installed Java class. `DT_FWD` transmits traction to the front axle, `DT_RWD` - to the rear; the combination of flags and `DT_AWD`/`DT_4WD` work as all-wheel drive with the share of `drive_front` (if there is no value of 50%). This corresponds to the method of distributing thrust in `Transmission.fillDynoData`; The native differentials and locks have not yet been reproduced. For installed turbo and mechanical superchargers, the operating speed range, `P_turbo_max` and wastegate pressure are read; boost increases torque and changes with a delay.

Important: `DynoData.getTorque()` and `calcDyno()` in `parts/scripts/src/DynoData.java` are declared **native**, their algorithm is not in the Java files. Therefore, the torque curve still remains a limited estimate based on displacement (9 bar BMEP), with a shape based on rpm and correction for boost. Engine inertia, clutch, automatic shifting, tires and turbo lag are now also approximate models, and not an exact copy of the native SLRR code. The tire parameters from the original `Tyre.java` are not yet used: in the current standard assembly, the rims and tires are not installed as separate units. The kinematic geometry and independent unsprung masses are also not brought to the original physics. The stabilizer operates by a simple pair of opposing forces rather than the original hinges.

For movement, a general empirical traction transmission has been introduced: a coefficient of 3.6 to 100 km/h smoothly decreases to 0.82 to 145 km/h. Clutch capacity is scaled with the engine torque estimate instead of being the same number for all motors. So the stock Baiern reaches 100 km/h in about 6.10 s versus 6.109 s in the original; a quarter and one mile are also close in time. This calibration is valid for all configurations, but has so far been confirmed by only one standard and should not be taken as the original physical formula of the game. With automatic upshift, the gear is selected based on road speed, and the revs are adjusted to the new gear during the shift; this eliminates the previous cascade of 1→2→3→4 at one speed. The mass of the entire car is now taken from the CFG physical bodies without conditional minimum masses for the installed suspension and wheels.

Hard meshes of mechanical parts are rendered using the original SCX and mounts. The Java brake classes define four `renderID_FL/FR/RL/RR`: this is one type of part with different meshes at the corners. When installing, the appropriate RID is selected, otherwise the right brake would use the left mesh and protrude on the outside of the disc. The strut, spring, shock absorber and brake at each corner move and rotate with the wheel load-bearing assembly as the suspension travels; The rotation of the tire around the axis is not transmitted to them. This is still the movement of the group as a single whole, without independent kinematics of each joint. The stabilizer remains attached to the body. For a separated engine, the mass is summed over the nested parts, and the total collider covers their original meshes; **one** rigid body is solved. The separated wheel receives a separate rigid body and interacts with the floor, obstacle and body. While nodes are installed, their own visual bounding boxes do not create unnecessary contacts. In the scene, the **"Internal Assemblies"** checkbox hides the body; the **“Engine separation”** and **“Left strut breakage”** buttons set 0% to the corresponding unit for reproducible inspection. `R`/"Reset" restores the build and state. The stock rims and tires, listed in Java as separate `stock_parts_list_RGear_wheels/tyres`, have not yet been assembled: the current visible wheel assembly is taken from the chassis stub CFG. Therefore, the exact silhouette of the wheel relative to the wing has not yet been confirmed.

The CFG of one part can contain several `render` commands: at the front pillar, the first selects `FL_strut.scx`, the second selects `FL_lower_arm.scx`; We used to lose the lower control arm. The rear arms also have several visual meshes. Now all available external SCX of such a CFG are rendered as one part with a common pose and state. Resources without external SCX are not yet decoded.

## Play

`npm test` checks arithmetic and branch selection without executing the source, reading four render brake IDs, `setSlotPos`, `bonepos` and `linked`, changing the support, damping, brakes and traction, the movement of visible parts with the wheel when the stabilizer is stationary, as well as the integral separation of the engine and the fall of the wheel with the remaining stance. `npm run verify:components` reads the real local setup and checks the 72 parts installed, the wheel position after `setSlotPos`, the top of the shock being aligned with the spring axis at all four corners, the four corresponding brake meshes, the 20 engine nodes, the actual spring/shock values and damage response. `npm run build` checks strict TypeScript and browser build.
